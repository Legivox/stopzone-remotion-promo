# Stopzone Promo — Review Storyboard

**Format:** 9:16 vertical master, 16:9 landscape adaptation  
**Timeline:** 5 scenes × 6 seconds = 30 seconds at 30 fps  
**Visual direction:** dark near-black base, restrained red accent, real app screenshots, close product framing, subtle camera drift, controlled kinetic type, and scene changes motivated by narration.

## Scene 1 — The problem (00:00–00:06)
Narration: “Ever looked up… and wondered if your stop is next?”
Visual: establish Stopzone immediately. Use the actual home screen as the hero asset. Let a thin route line draw behind it; headline enters with a measured rise and settles. Avoid a tiny floating phone and excessive empty space.

## Scene 2 — Set the zone (00:06–00:12)
Narration: “Drop a pin. Choose how early you want to be alerted.”
Visual: use the actual map/radius screen. Animate a subtle push-in and highlight the pin and zone; keep screen text large enough to read on a phone.

## Scene 3 — Relax (00:12–00:18)
Narration: “Then put your phone away… and enjoy the ride. Stopzone tracks your journey in the background.”
Visual: switch to the distance countdown screen. Slow camera drift and restrained distance emphasis. The movement should feel intentional, not like a looping screensaver.

## Scene 4 — The wake-up (00:18–00:24)
Narration: “When your stop is near, it wakes you. Even when your phone is on silent.”
Visual: hard, clean reveal of the real alarm screen. Use one short alarm accent and a restrained red pulse. Do not overuse shake, glow, or strobe effects. Validate the silent-mode claim before publication.

## Scene 5 — Brand close (00:24–00:30)
Narration: “Stopzone. Get off where you meant to. Download Stopzone on the App Store.”
Visual: actual icon, tagline, official App Store badge and verified destination. Hold the final frame long enough to read.

## Audio mix notes
- Keep narration clearly in front of music.
- Music should support the pacing rather than dominate it.
- Use a short alarm accent only at the wake-up reveal.
- Review on phone speakers and headphones; these are starting levels, not final mastering.

## Review status
This storyboard documents the intended direction. The current source uses local asset filenames and must not be considered fully portable until the required files are committed under `public/assets/` and `public/audio/` and the rendered MP4 is checked.
